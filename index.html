02重ねるハザードマップ — the hazard map portal

Every municipality in Japan publishes its hazard maps here: assumed flood extent and depth, landslide zones, and the sites designated for emergency evacuation. It is the map the people of Hitoyoshi could have looked at on 3 July 2020.

  1. Open the flood link above. 洪水浸水想定区域 — the assumed inundation area — is already on.
  2. Type 人吉市 or Hitoyoshi into the address box (住所を入力) and press Enter.
  3. Find two places: the city centre, and the 渡 (Watari) district downstream, in the narrow part of the valley.
  4. Read the colour. It is depth, not probability. The legend (凡例) gives the bands — darker is deeper.
  5. Switch on 指定緊急避難場所 — designated emergency evacuation sites.
  6. Click a site marker. A panel opens with eight hazard types, each marked valid or not valid for that site.

A site is designated hazard by hazard. The same building can be a valid refuge from a landslide and not a valid refuge from a flood. Nobody is hiding this — it is printed on the map. It only matters if somebody reads it before the night it counts.

YOUR TASK · 15 MIN

  1. Hitoyoshi city centre: what depth does the assumed map give?
  2. The Watari (渡) district: what depth?
  3. Find one designated evacuation site in or near Hitoyoshi that is valid for 崖崩れ (cliff collapse) and not valid for 洪水 (flood). Write down its name.
  4. Somebody is told to go to that site at 04:00 on 4 July 2020. What happens to them?

Question 3 is the one to spend time on. Question 4 has no written answer — say it out loud at your table.

03地理院地図 — GSI Maps

The national mapping agency's map viewer. We use one function of it: putting two maps side by side. What was assumed, next to what happened.

The two layers

What was assumed洪水浸水想定区域（想定最大規模） — the assumed inundation area for the largest flood the planners considered
What happened浸水推定図 — the estimated actual inundation, compiled at 13:00 on 4 July 2020, hours after the water peaked

If you want to build the comparison yourself

  1. Open maps.gsi.go.jp and find 機能 (Functions) in the top bar.
  2. Choose 地図の比較 (Compare maps), then either 並べて比較 — two maps side by side, panning together — or 重ねて比較 — one over the other with a slider.
  3. Load the assumed layer in one window and the 2020 layer in the other.
  4. Click anywhere to read 標高 — the ground elevation. In a valley this explains a great deal.

YOUR TASK · 12 MIN · WITH YOUR TABLE

  1. Name one place where the assumption was right.
  2. Name one place where the water went beyond the assumed area.
  3. Find somewhere the assumed map says shallow — under one metre — and the 2020 map says deeper. Where?

Use the settlement names from the map, not "over there". Two people pointing at different places while agreeing with each other is the classic failure of a coordination meeting.

04Tonight — find this flood from orbit

About 20 minutes, on your own, the evening of 2 October. No account, no code. Bring one screenshot for each question and five minutes of Session 2 is yours.

Sentinel-2Optical. It records sunlight reflected off the ground, so it sees roughly what your eye would see — and cloud stops it completely.
Sentinel-1Radar. It sends its own microwave pulse and measures the echo, so it works at night and through cloud. Calm water reflects the pulse away and returns almost nothing — it reads dark.

Getting to the right place and the right week

  1. Open either site. Search for Hitoyoshi, or type the coordinates 32.21, 130.75.
  2. Switch the mode to Find a Scene. A calendar appears — this is how you move through the archive.
  3. Set the calendar to July 2020. Each entry is one pass of the satellite over this valley.
  4. Open the cloud filter fully before you conclude anything. If it is set to "under 10% cloud" you will be shown an empty July and draw the wrong conclusion for the right reason.
  5. Change what you are looking at with the rendering list — Natural Colour, Colour Infrared, Short-wave Infrared, and the indices. On Sentinel-1, look for the water ones.
  6. The Swipe and Animate modes put two dates against each other. Usually more convincing than one image alone.

THREE QUESTIONS · ONE SCREENSHOT EACH

  1. In Sentinel-2, find the first image after 4 July 2020 in which you can actually see this valley — not cloud. What date is it?
  2. In Sentinel-1, look at the first week of July 2020. Can you see the water? Try a water index, and note where you put the threshold.
  3. Which of the two would have been any use to somebody making a decision at 05:00 on 4 July 2020?

If the archive does not reach back to 2020 at all, say so — that is a finding too, and it is worth more than a screenshot of the wrong year.

Do not look up what happened at the nursing home. It is one search away and it will spoil Session 2 for your whole table.

05uMap — where you draw your decisions

Session 2. A free, open mapping tool built on OpenStreetMap. No account, nothing to install. One link gets you in, and the map stays yours afterwards.

  1. Open the map link above in a normal browser. Chrome, Safari, Edge and Firefox all work.
  2. Click Edit at the top right, or press Ctrl + E.
  3. Find your table's layer: G1 drawings or G2 drawings.

One person draws at a time. Everyone else discusses. Two people editing the same map at once is allowed, but it is how features get lost.

The five things you will actually do

Do thisHow
Draw an areaDraw a polygon or Ctrl + P → click each corner → click the last corner twice to finish
Draw a lineAdd a polyline → click along the route → double-click to finish
Place a pointAdd marker → click the spot
Fill it inEvery feature needs three things: Layer (check it says your table), Name, Description
SaveSave, top right — then say "saved" out loud, so the other table can reload and see it

The mistake everyone makes: drawing on the wrong layer. uMap remembers whichever layer you used last, and it is often not yours. Look at the Layer field every single time before you save.

Writing a decision — 50 characters

Every shape carries a written decision in its Description field. Three things, in this order: what you decided · what time · what it costs you.

ExampleWhy
GoodL4 Watari 0455. 2 buses to Senjuen. Accept vertical.A decision, a time, and what was given up.
GoodHold brigade. Cannot reach Watari. Asked pref 0715.States the limit and the request. Someone else can act on it.
BadWe discussed the situation and decided to monitor it.No time, no action, no cost.
BadEvacuate everyone immediatelyWhich district? With what? Nothing is decided yet.

Fifty characters is long enough for a decision and too short for an excuse. "Wait and see" is allowed — but write it as a decision: "Standby only 2300. Recall staff 0200 if rain holds."

Naming

Start every name with your role, then say what it is: HQ- · EVAC- · SHEL- · FIRE-. Example: EVAC-L4 Watari 0455.

07Reference

Eight Japanese words that carry the whole of Session 1

洪水Flood浸水深Inundation depth
崖崩れCliff collapse土石流Debris flow
指定緊急避難場所Designated emergency evacuation site指定避難所Designated shelter (afterwards)
凡例Legend標高Ground elevation

指定緊急避難場所 is where you run to. 指定避難所 is where you sleep afterwards. Two different lists, and the difference cost lives in 2020.

Japan's five evacuation levels

L3高齢者等避難Older people and anyone who needs help leave now.
L4避難指示Everyone in the danger area leaves now.
L5緊急安全確保Too late to move safely. Protect your life where you are.

Level 5 is not a stronger Level 4. It is an admission that Level 4 came too late.

When something goes wrong

No Edit buttonYou opened the view-only link. Use the map link at the top of this page.
My shape vanishedIt is probably on another layer that is switched off. Turn all layers on and look again.
Wrong layerClick the feature, open its properties, change the Layer field, save. Nothing is lost.
Deleted by accidentDo not save. Reload without saving. If you already saved, tell the instructor — there is a backup.
A Japanese layer will not drawZoom in. Several of these layers only appear below a certain zoom level.
Nothing loads at allSay so immediately. Nothing in this exercise depends on the software.
